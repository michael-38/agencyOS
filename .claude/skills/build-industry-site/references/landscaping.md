# landscaping

## Visitor priorities
- Whether this company does *my* specific job — a patio, a wall, a drainage problem, a full
  redesign, or recurring mowing — and does it in my town.
- What it costs, at least as a range per project type or by lot size, before anyone has to talk.
- Proof the work gets finished: real completed projects in good light, before/after pairs, crews
  and trucks that look like a going concern.
- A clear process from site visit to finished build, because the fear is scope creep and being
  abandoned halfway.
- One tap to a quote request that lets them describe the property, or to the phone.

## Sections
### hero
- **Purpose:** Tell the visitor in one screen that this company does their kind of work, in their
  town, and can be reached now.
- **Required elements:**
  - The `<h1>`, naming the work and the service area as the source writes them.
  - An opening sentence that answers "do you do my job, near me?" without qualification.
  - The primary quote CTA, plus the tel link in the header.
  - A finished-project photograph, not a stock landscape.
- **Copy guidance:** Take the H1 from the source's own description of what it does; keep the place
  names exactly as written. Do not promise a response time, a price, or a start date the source
  does not state.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Hero photo: a finished project of the kind this company sells, in daylight"
### services
- **Purpose:** Let both buyers self-identify within the first two screens.
- **Required elements:**
  - The design/build work and the recurring maintenance work as separate, clearly labelled groups.
  - Each service named as the source names it, with one sentence on what the visitor gets.
- **Copy guidance:** Only services the source describes. A nav label with no supporting text is not
  a service. Say what the homeowner ends up with, not what the crew does.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Service photo: an example of this service completed"
### trust
- **Purpose:** Answer "will they finish?" before the visitor has to ask.
- **Required elements:**
  - Licensing, insurance, memberships, and years in business exactly as the source states them.
  - Reviews or a rating only when the source page carries those exact words or that exact number.
  - A labelled placeholder block when the source carries no proof, tagged and left failing.
- **Copy guidance:** Never soften a missing credential into a general claim of professionalism.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Team photo: crew and branded vehicles on a job site"
### gallery
- **Purpose:** Show the work, because this purchase is bought with the eyes.
- **Required elements:**
  - Completed projects grouped by the project types the source names.
  - Before/after pairs wherever the source presents work that way, captioned with the project type.
- **Copy guidance:** Captions name the project type and, where the source states it, the town.
  Never invent a location, a budget, or a timeline for a project.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Project photo: before/after of a project type the source names"
### process
- **Purpose:** Remove the fear of scope creep by making the sequence visible.
- **Required elements:**
  - Ordered steps from first contact to finished build, one sentence each.
  - The recurring or seasonal arrangement, when the source describes one.
- **Copy guidance:** Steps come from the source's own description of how it works. Do not invent a
  design phase, a deposit schedule, or a warranty.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Process photo: a site visit or a design review in progress"
### faq
- **Purpose:** Answer the questions that otherwise become a phone call the visitor never makes.
- **Required elements:**
  - Question-form headings, each answered in one to three self-contained sentences.
- **Copy guidance:** Answer only from the source; where the source is silent, write a short
  placeholder answer with no number, date, or guarantee in it.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - none
### contact
- **Purpose:** Capture a qualified request without a phone call.
- **Required elements:**
  - The tel link, the request form, and the service area in words.
  - Form fields that let the visitor describe the property and the job.
  - Hours and address when the source states them.
- **Copy guidance:** The service area is listed verbatim from the source — the town names are the
  proof, so they must be right.
- **Placeholder imagery labels:**
  - "Map placeholder: the service area as the source describes it"

## FAQ seeds
- Do you work in my town?
- Do you handle the design as well as the build?
- Can I get a quote without an in-person visit?
- Do you take on recurring maintenance as well as one-off projects?
- Are you licensed and insured?
- What happens at the first site visit?
- Do you work through the winter?
- Who looks after the plants once the work is finished?

## Vocabulary to use / avoid
- **Use:** landscaping, lawn care, mowing, clean-up, patio, paver, retaining wall, drainage,
  irrigation, sod, planting beds, grading, hardscape, softscape, site visit, estimate, quote,
  maintenance plan, the town and neighbourhood names the source uses.
- **Avoid:** "solutions" and "outdoor living experiences" (nobody searches for these); "affordable"
  and "competitive pricing" (a claim with no number behind it); "award-winning", "licensed and
  insured", "fully bonded", or any credential the source does not state; "free estimate" unless the
  source says the estimate is free; "we treat your home like our own".

## Design direction
- **Mood:** Capable and outdoors. This visitor is buying a physical result they will look at every
  day, so the page should feel like a portfolio with a phone number, not a brochure. It has to read
  as a real crew with real trucks — confident and plain, never corporate.
- **Palette:** A deep natural ground drawn from the client's own photography, warm neutrals for
  surfaces, and a single high-contrast accent held back for the quote action so it is never
  competing with the work. Greens are obvious here; if the client's photographs already carry the
  green, let the interface be the neutral and the photographs be the colour.
- **Type:** A grotesque or humanist sans for both display and body — this is not an editorial page.
  Display can be heavy and tight; body stays comfortable, because the maintenance buyer is often
  reading outdoors on a phone in sunlight.
- **Density and rhythm:** Generous. Large photographs, few words, a lot of air between blocks. The
  first screen carries the headline, one sentence, the CTA, and one photograph — nothing else.
- **Hero form:** Full-bleed photograph of finished work with the headline over it, or a split with
  the photograph taking the larger half. Never a gradient or an illustration.
- **Motion:** Restrained. A short fade-and-rise as sections enter, nothing on the photographs, and
  nothing at all when reduced motion is requested.
- **Proof:** Finished projects are the proof, so the gallery is a first-class section rather than a
  strip near the footer. Before/after pairs sit side by side at every width. Credentials are stated
  plainly as text, not as badges, unless the source shows a real badge.
- **Imagery:** Wide, daylight, in focus, showing the whole result rather than a detail. Crop to
  landscape ratios and let images run to the edge of the container. No filters, no duotone, no
  text baked into the image.
- **Avoid:** Stock photography of anonymous lawns; leaf and shovel icon sets; script fonts; a
  "trusted by" logo wall; dark overlays so heavy the work underneath disappears.

## Page architecture
- **Generate:** one page per project type the source describes with real substance (the design/build
  types and the maintenance offering are separate pages when both are described); one page per town
  the source explicitly names as served, only when the source says something specific about it; one
  FAQ page once there are three or more sourced questions.
- **Home page answers:** "Does this company do my kind of job, near me, and how do I start?"
- **Query intents:**
  - landscapers near me
  - who builds paver patios in <town>
  - lawn mowing service <town>
  - retaining wall contractor <town>
  - landscape design and installation <town>
  - how much does a patio cost
- **Entities to name:** every service by the source's own name; every town and neighbourhood the
  source says it serves; the licence or insurance statement exactly as written; the business name
  in full in the first sentence of the home page.
- **Internal linking:** the home page links to every service page by the service's own name; each
  service page links back to the home page and across to the areas it is offered in; area pages
  link to the services available there; the FAQ links to the service page each answer concerns.
