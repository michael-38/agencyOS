What to do now Find an obituary or service

A death has occurred. Here is exactly what to do.

Call us at (610) 555-0107 and we will take it from there. If you want to know what happens next, these are the five steps in order.

  1. Call the right number first

    If the death was expected and hospice is involved, call hospice. If it was unexpected, or you are unsure, call 911. In a hospital or nursing home, tell the nurse. Then call us. You do not need to wait for anyone’s permission.

  2. Call us at (610) 555-0107

    A funeral director answers, at any hour. We will ask where your person is, their name, and your name. That is all we need to begin.

  3. We come and bring them into our care

    Usually within two hours, day or night, in an unmarked vehicle. Two of our staff come. You are welcome to be present or not, whichever you need.

  4. We meet the next day, not tonight

    Arrangements take about ninety minutes, at our house or at yours. Nothing is decided tonight beyond burial or cremation, and even that can usually wait until morning.

  5. We handle the paperwork

    Death certificates, permits, Social Security notification, veterans’ benefits, the newspaper notice and the online obituary. We order certified copies for you. Most families need between six and ten.

What to bring to the arrangement meeting

None of this has to be perfect, and nothing is urgent enough to search the house at 3 a.m.

  • A recent photograph, and clothing if there will be a viewing
  • Their full legal name, date and place of birth, and Social Security number
  • Parents’ names, including their mother’s maiden name
  • Education completed and occupation, for the death certificate
  • Any pre-arrangement paperwork, cemetery deed, or life insurance policy
  • The DD-214 if they served, and if you cannot find it we will request it

What we can arrange

Burial, cremation, green burial, or something of your own design

Rather more than half of the families we serve now choose cremation, and almost all of them still hold a gathering, so every option below can be paired with a viewing, a service, or nothing at all.

Traditional burial service

$7,450

A viewing, a funeral service in our chapel or your place of worship, a procession, and a graveside committal. Includes our professional services, transfer into our care, embalming, dressing, the casket coach and the register book. Cemetery charges and the casket are additional.

Cremation with a memorial service

$3,650

Cremation at our own crematory on West State Street, where your person never leaves our care, followed by a memorial service in our gathering room or wherever you choose. Includes urn, register book, printed folders and the livestream.

Direct cremation

$1,895

Transfer into our care, all paperwork and permits, cremation, and return of the cremated remains in a temporary container. No service, no viewing. Families often choose this and hold a gathering themselves later.

Green burial

$4,200

No embalming, a shroud or a biodegradable casket, and burial at Laurelwood Conservation Burial Ground twenty minutes from Media. We have arranged green burials since 2016 and will walk you through what the ground there requires.

Celebration of life

$2,980

A gathering on your terms, at our house, a fire hall, a club, a park, a back garden, weeks after the death if that is when people can come. We handle coordination, sound, printing, catering referrals and the livestream.

Veterans’ services

No additional charge

Flag presentation, honor-guard coordination, the government headstone or marker, the VA burial allowance paperwork, and interment at Washington Crossing National Cemetery. A veteran’s grave, liner and perpetual care in a national cemetery are at no cost to the family.

Every service is livestreamed at no charge on a private link you can share, and the recording stays available for twelve months.

Deciding now, so nobody has to decide later

A pre-arrangement records what you want and, if you choose, fixes today’s price against the day it is needed. It is the single kindest thing we see people do for their children.

How people pay for it

  • Nothing at all. Record your wishes on file with no money down. We keep them and your family calls when the time comes.
  • A single payment into a Pennsylvania-approved funeral trust, which fixes today’s price for our services.
  • Three, five or ten-year payment plans, funded by an insurance assignment, with no interest and no medical examination.
  • An irrevocable trust, which Pennsylvania treats as exempt when assessing Medicaid eligibility. Ruth will explain exactly how that works for your situation.
  • Transferable. If you move away, the funds move with you to another funeral home.

Frequently asked questions

What it costs, how quickly you must decide, what to bring, and what happens with cremation and with veterans.

Someone has died at home. What do I do right now?

Call us at (610) 555-0107. A funeral director answers, day or night. If the death was expected and hospice is involved, call hospice first and then us; if it was unexpected, call 911 first. You do not have to decide anything else tonight. We will come, bring your person into our care, and talk about arrangements tomorrow.

How much does a funeral cost?

Direct cremation at Alderman & Sons is $1,895, cremation with a memorial service is $3,650, a celebration of life is $2,980, green burial is $4,200, and a traditional burial service is $7,450. Cemetery charges, vaults, monuments, flowers and newspaper notices are paid to other companies and are additional. Our full General Price List is downloadable from this page.

How soon do we have to decide anything?

Only two decisions are urgent: whether to bring your person into our care, and whether burial or cremation. Everything else (the day, the service, the casket, the obituary) can wait a day or two, and families who wait a day usually make better decisions. Pennsylvania places no deadline that requires you to decide overnight.

What should I bring to the arrangement meeting?

Bring a recent photograph, clothing for your person if there will be a viewing, and any pre-arrangement or insurance paperwork. For the death certificate we need their full legal name, date and place of birth, Social Security number, parents’ names including mother’s maiden name, education, and occupation. If they served, bring the DD-214 if you can find it. If you cannot, we will request it.

Can we still have a service if we choose cremation?

Yes, and most families who choose cremation do. You may have a viewing before the cremation, a memorial service after it with the urn present, or a celebration of life weeks later when relatives can travel. Cremation is a choice about disposition, not a decision to skip the gathering.

Do you handle veterans’ benefits?

Yes, at no additional charge. We file for the VA burial allowance, request the burial flag and the government headstone or marker, coordinate the honor guard, and arrange interment at Washington Crossing National Cemetery. A veteran’s burial in a national cemetery, including the grave, the liner and perpetual care, is at no cost to the family.

Can family who cannot travel watch the service?

Yes. Every service at Alderman & Sons is livestreamed at no charge on a private link you can share, and the recording stays available for twelve months so anyone who missed it can watch later.

Do we have to embalm?

No. Pennsylvania does not require embalming. It is generally needed only for a public viewing, for transport by some airlines, or where there will be a delay before burial. Refrigeration is available, and green burial does not permit embalming at all. We will tell you plainly whether your plan needs it.
