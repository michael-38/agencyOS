## Users

Homeowners, overwhelmingly on phones, and **overwhelmingly in emergency mode** — more so than any other vertical AgencyOS serves. A burst pipe, a backed-up sewer, an overflowing toilet, no hot water. They are stressed, standing in water, and will call the first plumber that looks legitimate. Reading is minimal; scanning is everything.

The secondary audience is planned work — water heater replacement, repiping, bathroom rough-in — but emergency searches drive the majority of traffic and therefore set the design.

This vertical carries a specific reputational headwind: the "shady plumber" stereotype. Homeowners arrive pre-suspicious about upselling and surprise pricing, so pricing transparency converts here in a way it does not elsewhere.

Vertical economics: Tier 2 target ($1K–$10K per job) in `lead-gen-target-industries.md`.

## Product Purpose

Convert into a phone call, fastest. Every second and every scroll between arrival and a tap on `tel:` is lost revenue. Secondary: capture planned work through a quote path.

## Positioning

[CLIENT] — the substantiable differentiator. Real candidates: genuine 24/7 live dispatch, upfront flat-rate pricing quoted before work begins, no overtime charges, a stated arrival window, licensed and background-checked technicians. **Only what can be proven.**

## Operating Context

- Search intent is acute and immediate; the visitor is often mid-crisis with water actively causing damage.
- Competitors compete on speed and price transparency. The stereotype means every legitimacy signal — license number, real photos, flat-rate promise — punches above its weight.
- Many calls happen from a phone held in one hand while the other deals with the emergency. One-handed, one-thumb reachability is a real constraint.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Click-to-call is the single most important element on the page.
- "24/7" may only appear if the business genuinely answers 24/7 with a human.
- Flat-rate and "no overtime charge" claims are pricing commitments and must come from the client.

## Evidence on Hand

[CLIENT] — license number, insurance, real reviews with source and count, technician and van photography, service area, pricing policy, response-time data.

**Absences future work must not fabricate:** license numbers, "available 24/7" claims, response-time guarantees, flat-rate promises, review counts, and years in business. Never generate a testimonial.

## Product Principles

1. **Speed to phone number is the whole design.** Everything else is secondary.
2. **Transparency defeats the stereotype.** Upfront pricing language converts better here than polish.
3. **Emergency sets the layout; planned work gets a lane, not the hero.**
4. **One-handed operation.** Thumb-reachable actions on mobile.
5. **Every claim is the client's, verified.**
